What your 12 minutes should show

  1. The problem and the user/system need
  2. Quantified workload and constraints
  3. Design alternatives you considered
  4. Selected architecture / data structures
  5. Key API, contracts, invariants
  6. Implementation highlights
  1. Testing strategy
  2. Benchmark / experimental evidence
  3. Results — with spread and conditions
  4. Limitations: what the evidence does not show
  5. What you would redesign under changed assumptions
Live demoFrom the tagged release: run the tests and one benchmark.

00:03

This is the engineering chain of the whole course in presentation form. Tell students that items 2, 3, 8 and 10 are where most marks are won or lost; item 6 should be short.

If a demo fails, the team explains why and shows committed results instead. A well-explained failure is better than a silent one.

What not to spend time on

Low value
  • Long background introductions
  • Explaining textbook algorithms (we know how a trie works)
  • Reading code line by line
  • Decorative slides without evidence
  • "Future work" lists without priorities
High value
  • One slide with the workload in numbers
  • The alternative you rejected, and why
  • One chart with spread, plus "what it does not show"
  • The invariant that almost broke
  • A negative result and what you learned

Presentation polish is not the primary goal.

00:06 Keep it brief; students should have prepared already. The point is to reassure them that plain slides are fine.

How the viva works

  • About 8 minutes per student, one at a time, in front of the team
  • Each student gets different questions
  • We start with your own contribution, then move beyond it: expect questions on a component a teammate wrote
  • You may be asked to open the repository and show where something happens
  • Teammates may not answer for you
Typical questions
  • Why this structure? What was the strongest alternative?
  • Which invariant matters most? Show where it is maintained.
  • What does your benchmark prove — and not prove?
  • What happens at 100× data, 90% writes, 32 workers?

00:08

Say: "The viva is not a memory quiz. 'I don't know, but here is how I would find out' is a reasonable answer. Guessing confidently is not."

Team result, individual understanding

  • All members receive the same project mark (20) and the same team presentation mark (2)
  • The viva (3) is individual
  • A student who cannot explain the team's design, or their own commits, receives a lower viva mark than teammates who can
What we look at
  • Answers to questions outside your own component
  • Ability to locate and explain code
  • Reasoning under changed assumptions
  • Commit history as supporting context — not as the only evidence
